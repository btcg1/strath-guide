Questions we had

What should I pack?

Hiking shoes or anything with real grip: Scotland is wet, rocky and slippery. Uniqlo Heattech (Extra Warm) saved my life multiple times. Bring at least two shopping bags, because every shop charges for plastic bags, and an extension cord or two. UK plugs are the same as Singapore's, so you only need adapters for EU trips. Download some shows too: data is patchy in the Highlands and on long journeys.

Are lectures recorded?

Not in our experience: each lecturer decides, and none of ours were. Attendance is different for every lecturer (QR code, password or paper), so don't skip anything in week 1. For MAE and EEE, attendance turned out to be very relaxed.

When do exam timetables come out?

Annoyingly late: about five weeks before exams, on Pegasus. That's the first week of November for semester 1 and early March for semester 2. Each one is a draft for a week, then final. In 2026/27 the assessment periods are 7–18 December and 19 April – 21 May (exam timetable FAQs). Book flights home with a flexible fare.

Which hall should I pick?

If you stay in Strath's halls, we'd go single-gender: the flats are a lot more spacious. Thomas Campbell Court has 4-bedroom flats sharing 1 bathroom (£146.16 a week). Birkbeck Court has 6-bedroom flats with 2 bathrooms (£146.16). Andrew Ure, Forbes and Garnett have rooms with a wash basin (£154.56), handy for washing small things and drying them on the radiator. Mixed flats mean more people: more fun, but a higher chance of a bad flatmate. Exchange accommodation.

What does a semester in halls cost?

A semester 1 let (14 weeks 2 days) is about £2,090 for a standard room or £2,210 with a basin. A semester 2 let (21 weeks 5 days) is about £3,170 or £3,360. Semester 1 rooms are limited, so apply early through the Accommodation Portal.

Anything weird about halls?

Fire drills every Wednesday >:( One was at 8 in the morning. Laundry at Lord Todd is about S$5 a wash and dry, and detergent isn't included (the app may give you a free cycle). Kitchens have a fridge, freezer, microwave, kettle and oven, but no air fryer. Cleaners do the shared areas a couple of times a semester. Checking out is relaxed: clear your room, donate pots and pans in the boxes by the halls, and drop your keys at Lord Todd.

What if something goes wrong?

Call 999 in an emergency. For urgent health advice that isn't an emergency, call NHS 24 on 111. Keep NTU's ISOS app on your phone and eRegister with MFA so Singapore can reach you if something happens.

What's the time difference with Singapore?

Singapore is 8 hours ahead of Glasgow from late October to late March, and 7 hours ahead the rest of the year, when the UK is on summer time. Plan calls home around that: lunch in Glasgow is evening in Singapore.

Can I rent privately instead of halls?

You can, but for one semester halls are much simpler. Halls are exempt from council tax. In a private flat, ask Strath for a student status letter for the council; whether you get the exemption is the council's decision. Strath accommodation.

How does the student card work?

Most students never need a physical card: once you register, your digital student card is in the Strathclyde app. If you want a plastic one, ask at the Teaching and Learning Building (it was free when we went).

Is the NHS free for me?

Don't count on it. NHS Scotland's free care for students is tied to a student visa, and you enter as a visitor. NTU's Group Travel Insurance (Chubb) covers sanctioned exchange travel, so read what it includes.

Which SIM card?

Lebara if you'll travel in Europe: 100 GB for £20 per 30 days, with EU roaming included. VOXI (Vodafone) has good eSIM deals, but roaming in Europe needs a pass (£2.60 a day, £15 for 8 days, £20 for 15 days). You can also buy SIM cards at most supermarket counters.

How much cash do I need?

About £100 for the whole exchange. 95% of places take cards, buses included, and when you split bills with international friends they'll often pay you back in cash anyway. Open a Monzo account early: Groupon kept rejecting our Singapore cards, and some memberships only take a UK Direct Debit.

How do buses work?

Every company does it differently, but they all take contactless cards like YouTrip or Wise. We ran into four systems: tap on and tap off like in Singapore; tap on for one flat fare however far you go; tell the driver your stop and tap what they key in; and book online ahead, mostly for long routes. Under 22 and living in Scotland? Buses are free with a National Entitlement Card.

Everyone jaywalks, but the traffic lights work very differently from Singapore's, so watch them for a few days first.

Where do I buy groceries?

ALDI (High Street) and LIDL (Duke Street) are the closest. Both are cheap with lots of own-brand stuff; LIDL is a bit more of a walk but has better stuff, so get the LIDL Plus app. Savers sells proper brands (Pantene, Dove, TRESemmé) for dirt cheap, and Poundland is the cheapest of the cheap. Chung Ying (254 Dobbie's Loan) is an Asian supermarket: kinda overpriced, but a dream. Primark for cheap clothes, Mountain Warehouse for hiking gear.

Which meal deal?

Tesco is £3.85 with Clubcard and has the best quality. Boots at Buchanan Galleries was the cheapest we found, with a clearance shelf. At Co-op take the Ginsters Cornish pasty as your snack: it's as filling as a main. Greggs throws in a free sausage roll with a cold Sandwich Deal if you show Student Beans. M&S is the fancy one.

Is National Trust for Scotland worth it?

Membership is £6.60 a month, or £3.80 on the Young rate, and gets you into every NTS castle, house and garden. Entry is usually £15–20, so it pays off fast, but you can't cancel during the first year, so it's really £79.20 (£45.60 Young). Under 22 with a National Entitlement Card? Historic Environment Scotland castles cost just £1.

What British food is worth ordering?

Gonna be honest, you don't come to the UK for the food. But: cullen skink (smoked haddock soup), steak and ale pie, shepherd's or cottage pie, haggis neeps and tatties (I love haggis), and sticky toffee pudding, the best thing Britain ever invented. Deep-fried Mars bar too; everyone hates me for saying this, but it's actually so good. Have Sunday roast only at a carvery or a good steakhouse. Chippies are a bit of a scam once you add sauces and sides (a meal can pass £20), but go once for the vibe. "Supper" means it comes with chips.

Best hikes?

Old Man of Storr on Skye was the most scenic, if the wind lets you up. Goatfell on Arran if you take the first ferry over and the last one back. Arthur's Seat in Edinburgh is easy and scenic, but the last bit to the very top is bare rock and a bit sketchy. We didn't make it up Ben Nevis.

Where can I see the northern lights?

Not really in Glasgow: too much light pollution. I tried at the Necropolis on a high-activity night and it was 90% cloudy. Calton Hill in Edinburgh sometimes works. Shetland is the best bet in Scotland: NorthLink's overnight ferry from Aberdeen, from £29.50 one way (2027 low season). Go early in the semester, while the nights are long. Iceland, Norway and Finland work too, but they're expensive.

What do people do in the evenings?

The Stand's Sunday improv is £1 for students. Concerts, festivals and musicals here are often cheaper than in Singapore. Kitty O'Shea's is a cool Irish pub with live music, and Maggie's does line dancing and rodeo nights. On campus there's Drag Bingo, Therapets, boxing and spin classes, and Strath Active events like UV badminton. Waterstones and the West End's secondhand bookshops for slow days.
