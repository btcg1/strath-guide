Semester at Strathclyde

Going to Strathclyde? Here's what we learnt about applying, course matching, the ETA, money, halls and daily life in Glasgow.

Start with applyingTrips and places

In the photo: University of Strathclyde

We spent semester 1 at Strathclyde in 2025. Here's what we'd tell a friend going next.

Start here for the stuff you'll need, in order: applying through NTU, course matching, getting into the UK, money, halls, food and the questions we kept asking each other. We checked prices and dates against the official pages in October 2026. Use the links to check again before you book.

The Travel tab is the fun part: the 24 trips we managed in one semester, 36 places with prices and opening times, and way too many photos. Strath is in an amazing spot for travelling, so make the most of it.

Apply

You apply through NTU's GEM Explorer on the GEM Mobility Portal. You need to have finished two NTU semesters, have a CGPA of 3.3 or above, and not have been on an in-person exchange before. You rank three universities, and NTU allocates places by CGPA band, citizenship, your ranking and how many spots each school has.

  • Strathclyde takes nominations 1 March – 15 May for a September start and 15 September – 15 October for January. NTU sends your nomination. Then keep an eye out for Strath's email.
  • Then you apply to Strath online with your passport, NTU transcript and class choices.

Match courses

Course matching is the part everyone panics about. Find past matches in CourseFinder, then submit each one in NTU's online course matching system (both need your NTU login).

  • Pick from Strath's exchange class catalogue and check for timetable clashes yourself.
  • Officially Strath allows classes from at most two departments. Honestly, don't stress too much. Strath's coordinators know NTU course matching can be a headache, and they're flexible as long as most of your classes are in your primary department. One of us (MAE) had MAE, EEE and DMEM classes approved.
  • Exchange grades count towards your AUs, not your CGPA.

Before you fly

Get a UK ETA before you fly: £20 on GOV.UK or the UK ETA app. It usually comes through within a day, but give it 3 working days. It lasts 2 years.

  • You enter as a Standard Visitor, which lets you study for up to 6 months but not work, not even an unpaid internship.
  • Before you leave, do NTU's briefing, indemnity letter, ISOS app and Travel Registry declaration, and eRegister with MFA for the trip.
  • At the border, carry your acceptance letter, accommodation details and proof of funds. Keep your boarding pass afterwards too.

Settle in

From the airport, take the Airport Express 500. It runs every 10 minutes, 24 hours a day, to Buchanan Bus Station, about a 15-minute walk from the halls. Collect your keys at Lord Todd. After you collect your keys, check the free market of cutlery, plates, decor and storage left by past students. Take what you need.

  • Week one: a UK SIM, a Railcard, Monzo, and the ESNcard at the society fair. Get it early: it sold out last time and wasn't restocked until the end of the semester.
  • Then head to TJ Hughes in the St Enoch Centre for bedding, pans and a laundry basket. Split an Uber back four ways and it's cheap.
  • Go to every class in week 1. Every lecturer takes attendance differently: QR code, password or paper.

Questions we had

What should I pack?

Scotland is wet, rocky and slippery, and the weather changes all the time. Here's what we were glad we packed:

  • Hiking shoes, or any shoes with a good grip.
  • Uniqlo Heattech (Extra Warm). You'll wear it almost every day.
  • Two or more shopping bags. Every shop charges for plastic bags.
  • Extension cords. UK plugs are the same as Singapore's, so you only need adapters for EU trips.
  • Downloaded shows. Data is patchy in the Highlands and on long journeys.
Are lectures recorded?

Not in our experience. Each lecturer decides, and none of ours were.

Attendance is different for every lecturer (QR code, password or a paper sheet), so don't skip anything in week 1. For MAE and EEE it turned out to be very relaxed.

When do exam timetables come out?

Annoyingly late: about five weeks before exams, on Pegasus.

  • Semester 1: first week of November (exams 7–18 Dec in 2026/27).
  • Semester 2: early March (exams 19 Apr – 21 May 2027).

Each timetable stays in draft for a week before it's final (exam timetable FAQs). Book flights home with a flexible fare.

Which hall should I pick?

If you stay in Strath's halls, we'd go single-gender: the flats are a lot more spacious.

  • Thomas Campbell Court: 4-bedroom flats sharing 1 bathroom, £146.16 a week.
  • Birkbeck Court: 6-bedroom flats with 2 bathrooms, £146.16 a week.
  • Andrew Ure, Forbes and Garnett: rooms with a wash basin, £154.56 a week. Handy for washing small things and drying them on the radiator.

Mixed flats have more people. That can be fun, but there's also more chance of a flatmate you don't click with. See exchange accommodation.

What does a semester in halls cost?

At 2026/27 prices:

  • Semester 1 let (14 weeks 2 days): about £2,090 for a standard room, £2,210 with a basin.
  • Semester 2 let (21 weeks 5 days): about £3,170, or £3,360 with a basin.

Semester 1 rooms are limited, so apply early through the Accommodation Portal.

Anything weird about halls?
  • Fire drills every Wednesday, sometimes as early as 8 am.
  • Laundry at Lord Todd is about S$5 a wash and dry. Detergent isn't included; the app may give you a free cycle.
  • Kitchens have a fridge, freezer, microwave, kettle and oven, but no air fryer.
  • Cleaners do the shared areas a couple of times a semester.
  • Checking out is relaxed: clear your room, donate pots and pans in the boxes by the halls, and drop your keys at Lord Todd.
What if something goes wrong?
  • 999 for emergencies.
  • 111 (NHS 24) for urgent health advice that isn't an emergency.
  • Keep NTU's ISOS app on your phone, and eRegister with MFA so Singapore can reach you.
What's the time difference with Singapore?

Singapore is 8 hours ahead of Glasgow from late October to late March, and 7 hours ahead the rest of the year, when the UK is on summer time.

Lunch in Glasgow is evening in Singapore, so that's the easy time to call home.

Can I rent privately instead of halls?

You can, but for one semester halls are much simpler, and halls are exempt from council tax.

In a private flat, ask Strath for a student status letter for the council. Whether you get the exemption is the council's decision. See Strath accommodation.

How does the student card work?

Most students never need a physical card: once you register, your digital student card is in the Strathclyde app.

If you want a plastic one, ask at the Teaching and Learning Building. It was free when we went.

Is the NHS free for me?

Don't count on it. NHS Scotland's free care for students is tied to a student visa, and you enter as a visitor.

NTU's Group Travel Insurance (Chubb) covers sanctioned exchange travel, so read what it includes before you fly.

Which SIM should I get?
  • Lebara if you'll travel in Europe: 100 GB for £20 per 30 days, with EU roaming included.
  • VOXI (Vodafone) has good eSIM deals, but roaming in Europe needs a pass: £2.60 a day, £15 for 8 days, £20 for 15 days.
  • You can also buy SIM cards at the counter of almost any supermarket.
How much cash do I need?

About £100 for the whole exchange. 95% of places take cards, buses included, and when you split bills with international friends they'll often pay you back in cash anyway.

Open a Monzo account early: Groupon kept rejecting our Singapore cards, and some memberships only take a UK Direct Debit.

How do buses work?

Every company does it differently, but they all take contactless cards like YouTrip or Wise. We ran into four systems:

  • Tap on and tap off, like in Singapore.
  • Tap on for one flat fare, however far you go.
  • Tell the driver your stop and tap the fare they key in.
  • Book online in advance, mostly for long routes.

Under 22 and living in Scotland? Buses are free with a National Entitlement Card. Everyone jaywalks here, but the traffic lights work differently from Singapore's. Give yourself a few days to get used to them.

Where do I buy groceries?
  • ALDI (High Street) and LIDL (Duke Street) are closest. LIDL is a bit more of a walk but has better stuff, so get the LIDL Plus app.
  • Savers for proper brands (Pantene, Dove, TRESemmé) for very little. Poundland for the cheapest of the cheap.
  • Chung Ying (254 Dobbie's Loan): an Asian supermarket. A bit pricey, but it has the Asian groceries you'll miss.
  • Primark for cheap clothes and shoes, Mountain Warehouse for hiking gear.
Which meal deal?

A meal deal is a main, a snack and a drink for about £4.

  • Tesco: £3.85 with Clubcard, and the best quality.
  • Boots (Buchanan Galleries): the cheapest we found, plus a clearance shelf.
  • Co-op: take the Ginsters Cornish pasty as your snack. It's as filling as a main.
  • Greggs: a free sausage roll with a cold Sandwich Deal if you show Student Beans.
  • M&S: the fancy one.
Is National Trust for Scotland worth it?

Membership is £6.60 a month, or £3.80 on the Young rate, and gets you into every NTS castle, house and garden. Entry is usually £15–20, so it pays off fast.

The catch: you can't cancel during the first year, so it's really £79.20 (£45.60 Young). Under 22 with a National Entitlement Card? Historic Environment Scotland castles cost just £1.

What British food is worth ordering?

Honestly, you don't come to the UK for the food. But these are worth ordering:

  • Cullen skink (smoked haddock soup).
  • Steak and ale pie, and shepherd's or cottage pie.
  • Haggis, neeps and tatties. Try it at least once; some of us loved it.
  • Sticky toffee pudding: the best thing Britain ever invented.
  • A deep-fried Mars bar, if you dare. Our group was very split on this one.
  • Sunday roast, but only at a carvery or a good steakhouse.

Chippies are a bit of a scam once you add sauces and sides (a meal can pass £20), but go once for the vibe. "Supper" means it comes with chips.

Best hikes?
  • Old Man of Storr (Skye): the most scenic, if the wind lets you up.
  • Goatfell (Arran): take the first ferry over and the last one back.
  • Arthur's Seat (Edinburgh): easy and scenic, but the last bit to the very top is bare rock and a bit sketchy.
  • Ben Nevis: we didn't make it, but heard great things.
Where can I see the northern lights?

Not really in Glasgow: there's too much light pollution. One of us tried at the Necropolis on a high-activity night, and it was 90% cloudy. Calton Hill in Edinburgh sometimes works.

Shetland is the best bet in Scotland: NorthLink's overnight ferry from Aberdeen, from £29.50 one way (2027 low season). Go early in the semester, while the nights are long. Iceland, Norway and Finland work too, but they're expensive.

What do people do in the evenings?
  • The Stand's Sunday improv: £1 for students.
  • Concerts, festivals and musicals, often cheaper than in Singapore.
  • Kitty O'Shea's for live music, and Maggie's for line dancing and rodeo nights.
  • On campus: Drag Bingo, Therapets, boxing and spin classes, and Strath Active events like UV badminton.
  • Slow days: Waterstones and the West End's secondhand bookshops.
