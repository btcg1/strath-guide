Fourteen weeks, 24 trips

Strath is in an amazing spot for travelling: four stations are a walk from the halls. Every pin is a trip we took in semester 1, 2025, from Oban on 14 September to North Wales the week before Christmas. Drag the slider through the weeks.

  1. Oban & Kilchurn

    14 September 2025. ESN day tour.

    One ESN day tour we'd recommend: Oban under McCaig's Tower, with Inveraray and Kilchurn Castle on the way.

  2. York

    18 September – 21 September 2025. Train from Glasgow Central.

    Medieval walls, Clifford's Tower and the National Railway Museum.

  3. Glenfinnan

    21 September 2025. ESN day tour.

    ESN day tour to the Harry Potter viaduct, via Luss on Loch Lomond and Fort William.

  4. Falkirk

    25 September 2025. Train from Queen Street.

    A very therapeutic canal walk from the Falkirk Wheel to the Kelpies.

  5. Inchcolm Island

    27 September 2025. Train, then Maid of the Forth ferry.

    An abandoned abbey on an uninhabited island. The boat leaves from Queensferry, a beautiful seaside town under the Forth Bridge.

  6. Loch Ness

    28 September 2025. ESN day tour.

    It's a long ESN day, but you get Glencoe, the Commando Memorial, Fort Augustus and Urquhart Castle.

  7. Arbroath

    29 September 2025. Train from Queen Street.

    Walk the coast for red sandstone cliffs and sea stacks.

  8. Hamilton Park

    30 September 2025. Train from Central.

    Glasgow's nearest racecourse.

  9. Stirling

    2 October 2025. Train from Queen Street.

    The castle and Wallace Monument are both up very steep hills. Give yourself more time than you think.

  10. Isle of Skye

    4 October – 5 October 2025. ESN weekend tour.

    Our best weekend. Eilean Donan, Portree, Sligachan and the Old Man of Storr, the most scenic hike we did.

  11. Culross

    10 October 2025. Stagecoach bus.

    A small town that looks like the Italian countryside.

  12. Dunnottar & Stonehaven

    11 October 2025. Train to Stonehaven.

    The clifftop castle ruin is a coastal walk from Stonehaven.

  13. St Andrews

    12 October 2025. Direct bus from Buchanan.

    Go on a Sunday, when the Old Course opens to the public. Cheesy Toast Shack and Jannettas are so good.

  14. Isle of Arran

    15 October 2025. Train, then CalMac ferry.

    Absolutely beautiful and close to school. First ferry over, last ferry back. We can't recommend it enough.

  15. New Lanark

    17 October 2025. Train to Lanark.

    A surreal mill village by the Falls of Clyde, with a steep walk from the station.

  16. Edinburgh

    18 October 2025. Flixbus or train.

    The castle, Arthur's Seat, the museum and the Saturday market.

  17. Loch Lomond & Hill House

    24 October 2025. Train to Balloch and Helensburgh.

    Autumn on the loch, then Mackintosh's Hill House.

  18. Aberdeen

    25 October – 29 October 2025. Train from Queen Street.

    Footdee, the beach, the university and Crathes Castle.

  19. East Linton & North Berwick

    31 October 2025. Train to East Linton.

    Preston Mill, Tantallon Castle and the beach at North Berwick.

  20. Argyll Forest

    1 November 2025. Day trip via Dunoon.

    We went via Dunoon and Glenbranter to the Rest and Be Thankful.

  21. Dunkeld & Birnam

    2 November 2025. Train from Queen Street.

    One of our favourite places in Scotland: the Hermitage, a 600-year-old oak and the river.

  22. Northern Ireland

    20 November – 24 November 2025. Coach and ferry from Buchanan.

    Belfast, the Giant's Causeway, the Dark Hedges and Derry.

  23. Inverness, Aviemore & Wick

    28 November – 30 November 2025. Train from Queen Street.

    Culloden, Fort George, Rothiemurchus (outdoor heaven) and the far north coast at Wick.

  24. Chester & North Wales

    16 December – 23 December 2025. Train from Central.

    Chester, Conwy, Llandudno, Snowdonia and Shrewsbury.

Questions about trips

How do buses work?

Every company does it differently, but they all take contactless cards like YouTrip or Wise. We ran into four systems:

  • Tap on and tap off, like in Singapore.
  • Tap on for one flat fare, however far you go.
  • Tell the driver your stop and tap the fare they key in.
  • Book online in advance, mostly for long routes.

In Glasgow, First Bus singles cost at most £2 since 7 September 2026, and the Subway takes contactless: £1.80 a ride, never more than £3.40 a day if you use the same card or phone.

Under 22 and living in Scotland? Buses are free with a National Entitlement Card. Everyone jaywalks here, but the traffic lights work differently from Singapore's. Give yourself a few days to get used to them.

Is National Trust for Scotland worth it?

Membership is £6.60 a month, or £3.80 on the Young rate, and gets you into every NTS castle, house and garden. Entry is usually £15–20, so it pays off fast.

The catch: you can't cancel during the first year, so it's really £79.20 (£45.60 Young). Under 22 with a National Entitlement Card? Historic Environment Scotland castles cost just £1.

Best hikes?
  • Old Man of Storr (Skye): the most scenic, if the wind lets you up.
  • Goatfell (Arran): take the first ferry over and the last one back.
  • Arthur's Seat (Edinburgh): easy and scenic, but the last bit to the very top is bare rock and a bit sketchy.
  • Ben Nevis: we didn't make it, but heard great things.
Where can I see the northern lights?

Not really in Glasgow: there's too much light pollution. One of us tried at the Necropolis on a high-activity night, and it was 90% cloudy. Calton Hill in Edinburgh sometimes works.

Shetland is the best bet in Scotland: NorthLink's overnight ferry from Aberdeen, from £29.50 one way (2027 low season). Go early in the semester, while the nights are long. Iceland, Norway and Finland work too, but they're expensive.
